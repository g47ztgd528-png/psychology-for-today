The floor is rarely the binding constraint. At typical psychology-niche RPM, hitting $10,000/month requires far more daily views than the 10M/90-day eligibility pace — so clearing the threshold isn't usually the risk. 10-Video-Daily Workflow, Built for the Anti-Template Rules

Since July 2025, YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (renamed from "repetitious content") blocks monetization for three categories — and psychology channels are especially exposed to the third one. Tubefilter

01

Generic or repetitive content

Templated storylines, minimal variation across videos, low educational value, little original commentary, or AI content built on generic templates that reads as mass-produced.

02

Unsatisfying or off-putting content

Emotionally manipulative formulas, formats that make videos feel interchangeable, or content engineered purely to shock — including stitched, unrelated AI clips with no narrative logic.

03

AI personas on sensitive topics

An AI presented as a human expert giving health, legal, financial, or political advice is disqualified from monetization outright. A psychology channel using an "AI therapist" persona to dispense advice sits squarely in this category.

Why this matters most for psychology content

Mental health is an explicitly named sensitive-advice category. Any format where an AI-generated host appears to be "a licensed therapist" or "a psychologist" giving direct advice is a monetization risk regardless of view count. Safer structures: a disclosed human host or clearly-labeled AI narrator delivering commentary and education (not clinical advice), citing named studies or real cases, with visible disclaimers that the channel is not a licensed provider.
